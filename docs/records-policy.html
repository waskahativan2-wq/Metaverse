A tamper-evident records framework for live documents, electronic signatures, email metadata, approvals, and ATIP/access requests.

Version and signature workflow

Never overwrite a signed or finalized record. Preserve the original, final signed copy, approval comments, signature evidence, and audit log.

Draft → Review → Approved → Signed → Archived

  • Record the signer’s name, role, identity-verification method, consent, and signing time.
  • Record the exact document version and a cryptographic hash.
  • Protect private signing keys and do not store them in ordinary email or application logs.

Email metadata

For relevant authorizations and business records, preserve sender, recipients, subject, timestamps, Message-ID, reply headers, attachments, attachment hashes, source mailbox, and related document IDs. Store the original message in an approved format such as .eml where appropriate.

ATIP readiness

  • Search by person, date, subject, project, or document ID.
  • Use preservation holds for access requests, investigations, audits, complaints, or legal matters.
  • Support review, redaction, third-party consultation, disclosure logs, and export.
  • Do not alter or delete potentially responsive records after a hold begins.

Security and access

  • Use immutable or write-once storage for finalized records.
  • Use hashes, versioned audit logs, encryption, MFA, backups, and synchronized time.
  • Apply least privilege and separate system administration from approval authority.
  • Permit auditors to read evidence without changing it.

Adaptable policy fields

Configure retention periods, authorized signers, approval hierarchy, data residency, privacy consent, community review, redaction rules, release authority, emergency access, and applicable Indigenous data-governance requirements.

OCAP® principles and community data rules should be defined with the applicable First Nation, organization, or data steward. A self-issued certificate or email alone does not establish authority.

Policy statement

Every approved OSIS document must be attributable, versioned, time-stamped, tamper-evident, retrievable, and linked to its approval and signature evidence. Email and metadata must be preserved according to applicable privacy, access-to-information, records-management, and community data-governance requirements.
