OSIS Live Document and Signature Records

A tamper-evident records framework for live documents, electronic signatures, approvals, email metadata, launch decisions, and ATIP/access requests.

Document record structure

Each record must preserve identity, ownership, version, classification, approvals, signatures, retention status, and integrity evidence.

Approval records also preserve the decision, rationale, release or artifact identifier, evidence links, open exceptions, rollback trigger, and next review date.

Version, evidence, and signature workflow

Never overwrite a signed, approved, archived, or held record. Preserve the original, final signed copy, approval comments, signature evidence, and audit log.

Draft → Review → Approved → Signed → Archived

  • Generate and retain a SHA-256 hash at creation and after each material change.
  • Link each version to its previous record and approval evidence; use previous_hash: null only for a documented initial version.
  • A change to a signed record creates a new version and requires re-review and re-signature.
  • Record the signer’s name, role, identity-verification method, consent, exact version, hash, and ISO-8601 UTC signing time.
  • Protect private signing keys and do not store them in ordinary email or application logs.
  • Store final evidence in immutable, write-once, or tamper-evident storage where practical.

Approval and launch controls

  • Identify the accountable owner, product, security, privacy, safety/moderation, legal/compliance, and operations reviewers as applicable.
  • Bind the approval to the exact release commit, artifact, configuration, migration identifiers, and evidence-package hash.
  • Record every exception with an owner, mitigation, due date, expiry date, and acceptance authority.
  • Do not mark a release Go until required evidence and signatures are complete.
  • Separate system administration from approval authority.

Email metadata

For relevant authorizations and business records, preserve sender, recipients, subject, timestamps, Message-ID, reply headers, attachments, attachment hashes, source mailbox, routing data, and related records. Email alone does not establish formal authority when a signed approval record is required.

ATIP readiness

  • Search by person, date, subject, project, or document ID.
  • Use preservation holds for access requests, investigations, audits, complaints, or legal matters.
  • Support review, redaction, third-party consultation, disclosure logs, and export.
  • Do not alter or delete potentially responsive records after a hold begins.

Security and access

  • Use least privilege, MFA, encryption, backups, synchronized time, and versioned audit logs.
  • Permit auditors to read evidence without changing it.
  • Protect signing keys and administrative credentials.
  • Test evidence retrieval and backup restoration periodically.

Adaptable policy fields

Configure retention periods, authorized signers, approval hierarchy, data residency, privacy consent, community review, redaction rules, release authority, emergency access, and applicable Indigenous data-governance requirements.

OCAP® principles and community data rules should be defined with the applicable First Nation, organization, or data steward. A self-issued certificate or email alone does not establish authority, consent, or ownership.

Policy statement

Every approved OSIS document must be attributable, versioned, time-stamped, tamper-evident, retrievable, and linked to its approval and signature evidence. Email and metadata must be preserved when they form part of the record.

This framework supports operational governance and lawful disclosure; it does not replace legal advice or jurisdiction-specific requirements.
