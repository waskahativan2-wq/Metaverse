# Ripple Effect Art+Music Curriculum — Reaction to Responsibility

A project-based art and music curriculum set in the OSIS universe. Students explore how one creative act — a song, a mural, a shared avatar skin — ripples outward through a community, and practice moving from impulsive **reaction** to considered **responsibility** as artists, performers, and audience members.

> This curriculum is an educational concept. Activities involving recording, likeness, public performance, or online sharing require qualified review of consent, privacy, child-safety, and content-rights obligations before use with real students. Original work only, or material with documented rights or licenses.

## Goals

Students will:

1. Create original art and music that expresses an idea about community, consent, identity, or ownership.
2. Trace how a creative work affects others — positively and negatively — using "ripple mapping."
3. Distinguish reaction (impulse, trend-chasing, copying) from responsibility (attribution, consent, licensing, care for the audience).
4. Practice asking permission, crediting creators, and giving and receiving respectful critique.
5. Reflect on accessibility and inclusion: who can experience their work, and who is left out?

## Age bands

| Band | Ages | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Junior | 8–11 | Cause and effect; sharing and crediting; kind feedback. |

## Lesson plans

### Lesson 1 — The First Ripple

- **Warm-up:** One student makes a sound or mark; the next responds; continue around the circle. Debrief: how did your choice change the next person's?
- **Art:** Collaborative circle mural — each addition must respond to a neighbor's mark without covering it.
- **Music:** Call-and-response rhythm circle; the leader changes, the group adapts.
- **Reflection:** Draw a ripple map: your act → who it touches → what they might do next.
- **Responsibility practice:** Name one way to make the next ripple better for the group.

### Lesson 2 — Whose Line Is It?

- **Art:** Compare "in the style of" studies with copied works; label influence vs. imitation.
- **Music:** Vary a shared melody (change rhythm, key, instrumentation) and discuss when a variation becomes a new work.
- **Responsibility practice:** Add an "inspired by" credit line to every piece; Junior bands use a simple credit stamp.
- **Discussion:** How does it feel when your work is copied without credit? (Links to OSIS theme: identity versus ownership.)

### Lesson 3 — Ask First

- **Art:** Portrait exercise — students may only draw a classmate who agrees; practice a plain-language consent ask.
- **Music:** Record ambient sounds and voices only from volunteers; learn why surprise recording is harmful.
- **Responsibility practice:** Use a simple permission slip (Junior) or consent checklist (Intermediate/Senior) for any featured person.
- **Rule:** No work may include a person's likeness, voice, or name without their permission.

### Lesson 4 — The Remix License

- **Art:** Build a collage from public-domain or openly licensed images; keep a source list.
- **Music:** Create a track using only cleared loops and original recordings; document each source and license.
- **Responsibility practice:** Complete a rights log: asset → source → license → how credited.
- **Discussion:** What may be reused freely, what needs permission, and what is off-limits? (Mirrors the Marketplace provenance rules.)

### Lesson 5 — Amplify Kindly

- **Art:** Design a poster for a cause the class chooses; check claims for accuracy and tone for respect.
- **Music:** Write a short song of hope or protest; workshop lyrics for honesty and for impact on listeners.
- **Responsibility practice:** Apply a "no dark patterns" check — no guilt trips, fear tactics, or false urgency to move an audience.
- **Reflection:** Persuasion respects the audience's ability to choose.

### Lesson 6 — The Audience Ripples Back

- **Art:** Gallery walk — leave feedback using the class protocol: observe, appreciate, question, suggest.
- **Music:** Listening circle — respond to a peer's piece with one specific observation before any judgment.
- **Responsibility practice:** Critique the work, never the person; the creator decides what to change.
- **Skill:** Separate the impulse to react ("I don't like it") from a responsible response ("the chorus loses energy because…").

### Lesson 7 — Design for Everyone

- **Art:** Create a piece that works in high contrast and by touch; add a short plain-language description.
- **Music:** Plan a performance with captions or a visual/conductive track, seating options, and a low-sensory space.
- **Responsibility practice:** Run an accessibility check: sight, hearing, mobility, sensory load, language.
- **Reflection:** A ripple only counts if people can actually receive it.

### Lesson 8 — Final Showcase: Reaction to Responsibility

- **Event:** Class exhibition and performance themed "Reaction to Responsibility."
- **Requirements for every exhibited work:**
  - Original or documented licensed sources, with credits displayed.
  - Consent on record for any featured likeness, voice, or name.
  - Accessible presentation: description, caption, or tactile element.
  - A one-sentence artist statement naming the intended ripple.
- **Close:** Students sign a class Ripple Pledge (below) and archive the showcase with a program listing every credit.

## The Ripple Pledge

1. I will make original work or use work I am allowed to use, and I will credit my sources.
2. I will ask before I feature any person's likeness, voice, name, or story.
3. I will think about who my work reaches and how it might affect them.
4. I will give feedback that helps and receive feedback with an open mind.
5. I will make my work as accessible as I can, and I will fix what I learn excludes someone.
6. When I react, I will pause — then choose the responsible ripple.

## Assessment

Assessment is formative and portfolio-based; there are no competitive rankings or prizes.

- **Process portfolio (40%):** Ripple maps, consent records, rights logs, drafts, and reflections.
- **Final work (30%):** Craft, clarity of idea, and fulfillment of the showcase requirements.
- **Responsibility in practice (20%):** Attribution accuracy, consent practice, critique quality, accessibility check.
- **Reflection (10%):** Artist statement and a short "reaction to responsibility" self-assessment.

## Safety, privacy, and accessibility

- No student personal data, images, or recordings leave the classroom without documented guardian consent where required.
- Recording devices and any digital tools must comply with the school's privacy and child-safety policies.
- Performances avoid flashing lights and sudden loud sounds; provide reduced-sensory alternatives.
- Content is age-appropriate; mature themes are excluded at every band.
- Every activity has a non-performing, non-visual, and non-auditory equivalent role (curator, program writer, sound engineer, documentarian).

## Rights and content clearance

- Use original student work, public-domain material, or clearly licensed educational resources only.
- Keep a rights log for every reused asset; do not use chart music, brand imagery, or character art without a documented license.
- Student creators keep rights in their own work; the school displays it only with permission and credit.
- Takedown: any student may withdraw their work from display at any time, no questions asked.

## Family and community connections

- Invite families to the final showcase with accessible scheduling and formats.
- Optional community ripple: partner with a local artist or musician to model professional attribution and licensing practice.
- Share the program (with credits) rather than student recordings; recordings only where consented.

## Alignment with OSIS themes

This curriculum enacts the OSIS principles in the classroom:

- **Identity versus ownership** — students keep ownership of their creations (Lesson 2, Lesson 8).
- **Consent and personal boundaries** — ask-first practice for likeness, voice, and recording (Lesson 3).
- **Creativity and creator rights** — attribution, provenance, and licensing (Lesson 4).
- **Community governance** — shared critique protocols and the Ripple Pledge (Lessons 6 and 8).
- **Accessibility and inclusion** — design-for-everyone standards (Lesson 7).
- **Good faith** — honest persuasion and no manipulative design (Lesson 5).
